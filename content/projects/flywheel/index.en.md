The aim was a laboratory-scale flywheel storage system to serve as a
demonstrator and as a test rig for teaching.

The prototype stores 200 Wh. The rotor, made of high-strength aluminium and
carbon-fibre-reinforced plastic, spins at 25,000 rpm in medium vacuum above
its critical speed, driven by a 3.5 kW permanent magnet synchronous machine.
The system can supply an island grid; one charge brews about five cups of
coffee.

I did the mechanical design including 3D model and drawings, selected
bearings and power electronics, estimated the thermal behaviour with a lumped
parameter model and designed the rotor dynamics with the finite element
method.
